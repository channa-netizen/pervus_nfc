01 / THE IDEA

WHY A CLOCK?

I've always been fascinated by the way time changes depending on what you're doing.

Five minutes can feel like an hour. An entire night can disappear in what feels like seconds. A song can pull you backward years. A moment with the right people can make everything else disappear completely.

So I built a clock that doesn't really give a shit what time it is.

Time Is an Illusion is meant to be touched, changed, played with, and experienced. There isn't a correct combination. There isn't a right way to look at it.

FUCK WITH IT.
MAKE IT YOURS FOR A MINUTE.
02 / INTERACTION

YOU'RE PART OF THE ART.

Those controls aren't there for decoration.

Push the buttons. Move things. Change the light. Make it weird.

Every person who interacts with it changes the piece, even if only for a moment. What you see doesn't have to be what the next person sees.

NO INSTRUCTIONS.
NO CORRECT COMBINATION.

Just curiosity and whatever happens next.

03 / UNDER THE HOOD

BUILT DIFFERENT.

It started as a grandfather clock.

What lives inside it now is a little less traditional.

Thousands of lights. ESP32s. Custom controls. Wireless communication. Code. Motion. A ridiculous amount of wiring. And more trial-and-error than I'm willing to admit.

Most of it exists because at some point I said:

“I BET I CAN
MAKE THAT WORK.”
⚡ ESP32 Microcontrollers running the chaos.
🌈 ADDRESSABLE LIGHT Individually controlled LEDs bring the cabinet alive.
🎮 CUSTOM CONTROL A controller built so the audience can take over.
🧠 WLED + CODE Effects, presets, communication and a lot of tinkering.

The exact guts keep evolving. That's part of the fun.

04 / THE POINT

STOP LOOKING.
START PLAYING.

I didn't want to build something people politely stood six feet away from.

I wanted the moment where someone notices it, gets curious, presses something, and then calls their friend over because they need to see this shit too.

ART GETS BETTER
WHEN YOU'RE ALLOWED
TO TOUCH IT.
05 / THE DORK

THE DORK
RESPONSIBLE FOR THIS.

I'm Chad.

I'm a dork who likes electronics, lights, code, building shit, and apparently second-guessing myself all the way into making cool things.

Calling myself an artist still feels a little weird.

But I make things that didn't exist before, put them in front of people, and hope they feel something—or at least get curious enough to push a button.

So... kinda an artist, I guess.

Mostly I'm just a person who keeps asking “what if?” and then makes the questionable decision to find out.
